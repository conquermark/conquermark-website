# Competitor comparison research

This research was collected from official public pages on August 17, 2026 and is intended to support careful website copy. It is not an endorsement or a legal/compliance opinion.

## ClickCease
Source: https://www.clickcease.com/

ClickCease publicly says it protects Google, Meta, and Microsoft Ads. Its public page highlights automatic blocking, quick installation, 24/7 support, Google/Meta API approval claims, multi-domain management, custom rules, traffic analysis, bot detection, real-time blocking, VPN/proxy exclusion, Performance Max protection, and reporting. The page also publishes customer and savings claims, including 15–20% spend-saving quotes and “50,000+ businesses.” These are ClickCease’s own public claims and should not be repeated as ClickMoat claims.

## ClickGuard
Sources: https://www.clickguard.com/ and https://www.clickguard.com/ad-engines-formats

ClickGuard publicly says it protects Google, Meta, and Microsoft Ads. It describes real-time detection, customizable rules, Google Ads API integration, protection for Search, Display, YouTube, Shopping, and Performance Max, IP exclusion, GCLID analysis, Meta Ads Manager sync, device fingerprinting, Microsoft Ads integration, cross-device protection, and unified reporting. Its homepage publishes “save up to 30%,” “$17M ad budget saved,” “9M clicks analyzed monthly,” “99.8% accuracy,” and “11K campaigns monitored monthly.” These are public ClickGuard marketing claims and should be attributed to ClickGuard or omitted from the ClickMoat page.

## ClickMoat
Source: https://clickmoat.com/

ClickMoat’s current public positioning is Google Ads first, with Meta Ads protection described as in development for September 2026. Supplied product materials show dashboard views for campaign performance, analytics, domain overview, platforms, fraud protection, keyword reports, and protection rules. Supplied rules include IP frequency, device frequency, VPN/proxy, geolocation, user-agent, referrer, time pattern, click pattern, and custom rules, with block, flag, or monitor actions. The comparison should describe ClickMoat as the product with a transparent, Google Ads-first workflow and visible evidence trail, not as the broadest platform.

## Safe comparison language

Use “publicly states,” “shown in supplied product materials,” “Google Ads live,” “Meta planned,” and “verify current availability” where appropriate. Avoid claiming that ClickMoat is cheaper, more accurate, safer, or better than competitors without approved evidence. Avoid copying competitor savings metrics. Include a “last checked” note and link each competitor name to its official source.

## Cookies and privacy content boundary

The landing page can explain the site’s intended cookie categories in plain language: essential cookies for navigation and security; analytics cookies only if analytics is actually enabled; preference cookies for settings; and marketing cookies only with explicit consent if those tools are added. The site should not claim that cookies are used for analytics or advertising unless the relevant scripts are enabled. The new section should state that this is a plain-language overview, not a substitute for a reviewed Privacy Policy or Cookie Policy, and should link to those documents once the client publishes them. It should also include a simple contact route for privacy requests.
